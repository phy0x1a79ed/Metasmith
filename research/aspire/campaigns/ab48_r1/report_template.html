How the tables were made

Two ASPIRE studies, each over the non-control samples of the lab: the eight AB48 cohorts, the Purify sequencing runs, the Nostoc and Anabaena cultures and Patrik's 2026 cultures. The V4 table holds every one of them; the V4–V5 table leaves out the three 2026 runs. Both ran on Sockeye as Metasmith workflows. The July report ran DADA2 once per sequencing run and joined runs by sequence afterwards. ASPIRE denoises every run together, so within a table one ASV identifier means one sequence across the whole lab.

Read QC and merge

fastp trims the 515F-Y and 926R primers by length. vsearch merges pairs and keeps merged reads of 245 bp or more at ≤1 expected error. For the V4 table every merged read is first cut to 250 bp.

Denoise

vsearch UNOISE3 (minimum size 3) and de novo UCHIME3 call ASVs over the pooled study. Reads map back at 99.9% identity.

Trim and classify

SINA aligns each ASV to SILVA 138.2 and trims it to V4–V5, or to V4 for the V4 table. QIIME 2's consensus vsearch classifier assigns taxonomy.

Curate

Samples need 5000 reads. Mitochondrial and contaminant ASVs are removed by BLAST, and an ASV must reach 0.5% in at least one sample.

Link to MAGs

barrnap finds 16S genes in the 24 dereplicated AB48 bins. Each ASV is paired with a bin by BLAST over its whole length.

Checks against the July lane

  • Over the AB48 samples, all 10 most abundant ASVs match a July DADA2 ASV by sequence, and four of the five dominant genera agree.
  • The dominant cyanobacterial ASV pairs only with bin 1-15, Sodalinema yuhuli, at 100% identity over its whole length. SILVA still calls this lineage Geitlerinema PCC-7105.
  • All 25 July ASVs that fall inside a binned 16S gene pair with that same bin here. An independent blastn agrees with every one of the ASV–MAG candidates.
