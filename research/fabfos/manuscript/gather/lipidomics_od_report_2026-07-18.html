The clones don't fight toluene at the membrane — they were already built for it

01 — the question

Does toluene change the clones' lipids differently than the parent's?

Two fosmid clones from the SCADC library tolerate 0.1% toluene; the EPI300 host and its empty-vector control do not. The lipidomics question is whether that tolerance is visible as a membrane-lipid signature — and whether toluene acts on the clone lipidome at all.

The catch is biomass. After six days in toluene the parent is knocked down to a fraction of its untreated density, while the clones are near-normal. Raw peak areas therefore mix two things — how much cell there is and what each cell's membrane is made of. Per the facility's own recipe (A. Szeitz), we divide every sample's peak areas by its culture OD600, giving lipid signal per unit biomass.

One OD is measured per culture; each culture is split into a cell-pellet (P) and supernatant (S) lipidomics fraction, so both fractions inherit their culture's OD. EPI300 does have toluene-treated samples, so its own tol-vs-ctrl change is measured directly.

02 — the correction that changes the story

The toluene signal only appears once you normalize to biomass

The earlier pass reported "toluene: 0 significant features." That was a normalization artifact. Median normalization forces every sample to the same total, which erases exactly the biomass-driven difference we care about. Raw areas swing the other way — the parent's collapse drags its totals down. Only OD normalization puts the comparison on a per-cell footing.

Method note. Primary normalization is the group geometric-mean OD, which is assumption-free about which replicate maps to which (it cancels the pairing) and far more robust for the high-variance toluene parent. Szeitz's literal per-replicate recipe gives the same central fold-changes. Every conclusion below holds under both.

03 — the answer

Toluene remodels the background lipidome. It barely touches the clones'.

Across both fractions the pattern is the same: the susceptible strains pour lipid into their surviving cells, spanning broad phospholipid classes (PE, PG, PC, PS, LPE) and the large unannotated fraction; the tolerant clones hold steady.

The strain × treatment interaction — the formal test of "does toluene act differently here" — is significant for 90–254 features comparing each clone to its parent, and its sign is uniformly one-way: the clone fails to show the parent's toluene-induced surge. The empty vector, by contrast, interacts least with the parent (31 features) — it behaves like the background it is.

In other words, toluene's lipid effect isn't reduced in the clones — it is largely absent. The clones keep the membrane books balanced while the parent runs an emergency deficit.

04 — the mechanism the lipidome supports

How the clones do it

If toluene isn't perturbing the clone lipidome, the protection must be upstream of the crisis the parent experiences. Three lines in the data point the same way, and one classic hypothesis is ruled out.

M1 Homeostasis, not response

The clones' per-biomass lipid amount and class mix are steady across toluene. Whatever keeps solvent away from the bilayer is already in place — no inducible remodeling is needed.

supported · primary signal
M2 A rebuilt baseline membrane

Even without toluene, 436 (pellet) / 647 (supernatant) features differ >2-fold in both clones vs parent — a constitutively remodeled lipidome. ~90% are unannotated: the insert-dependent "dark" lipids the founding fosmid hypothesis predicted.

supported · constitutive
M3 Two clones, two solutions

They tune membrane order oppositely: Clone 1 shifts to more saturated, shorter acyl chains (tighter packing); Clone 2 goes longer and more unsaturated. Different biophysical routes to the same tolerance.

hypothesis · sparse annotation
M4 Not membrane lysis

Lyso-lipids (LPE/LPC — the fingerprint of phospholipase-driven membrane damage) stay near zero everywhere, including the stressed parent. The parent's surge is lipid accumulation/synthesis, not hydrolytic breakdown.

ruled out

Putting it together

The lipidomics is most consistent with constitutive membrane engineering: the fosmid inserts reshape the resting membrane — a large, mostly uncharacterized set of lipids, plus a modest ordering shift each clone tunes its own way — so that 0.1% toluene never reaches the threshold that throws the parent into its stress-lipid excursion. The clones don't out-respond the parent to toluene; they were pre-adapted so the response is never triggered. That the two clones reach it by divergent acyl-chain strategies mirrors the modeling arm's read that Clone 1 and Clone 2 tolerate by different routes.

05 — hold this lightly

Caveats

  • Low-OD denominator. The parent's +toluene OD (0.11–0.29) is near the plate floor; part of its per-cell "increase" may be OD under-reading stressed cells rather than true lipid gain. The clone-vs-parent contrast is robust regardless.
  • Replicate pairing. OD-replicate ↔ lipidomics-replicate correspondence is not documented; the geometric-mean primary sidesteps it (conclusions match the per-replicate recipe).
  • Annotation ceiling. ~90% of the discriminating features are unannotated; the membrane-order read (M3) rests on the ~87 chain-resolved species and is hypothesis-grade. No cardiolipin in this table; PC calls are low-confidence (E. coli makes little PC).
  • Extraction physics (A. Alcázar-Magaña). Toluene can partition lipophilic species; not corrected here — though per-cell the respiratory quinones move up, not down, so the raw "quinone loss" was itself a biomass artifact.
  • n = 3, high toluene variance — per-feature FDR is conservative; the powered readouts are the per-biomass totals and the interaction test.
