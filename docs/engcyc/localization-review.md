# Open localization predictors for MAG annotation

Under a redistribution-safe licence filter, exactly one integrated prokaryotic localization predictor survives with the class resolution you need: **PSORTb 3.0 (GPLv3)**, wrapped for metagenomes as **PSORTm**. Everything else that is both accurate and current is either non-redistributable (SignalP 6, DeepTMHMM, DeepLocPro, Phobius, LipoP, TMHMM), web-only (CELLO2GO, BUSCA, PRED-TMBB2, BetAware-Deep, LocPred-Prok), or solves a sub-problem rather than localization (TMbed, DeepSig, SCAMPI2). The practical consequence is that the default CPU lane is a PSORTb container driven by a per-MAG envelope call, and the optional GPU lane is TMbed for membrane geometry plus, if you want full six-class output under a permissive licence, a classifier you train yourself on the public ePSORTdb labels.

## Predictors by problem

### Whole-cell localization

Prokaryotic localization prediction has had two distinct eras, and the licence filter unfortunately cuts across the boundary. The first era produced ensemble predictors that combine homology transfer, motif and profile scans, and per-compartment SVMs. PSORTb 3.0 is the mature representative: it is the first predictor to cover all prokaryotes including archaea and atypical envelopes, it refuses to guess when the ensemble score is below threshold, and in five-fold cross-validation on homology-reduced data it reported precision of 97.3% with recall 94.1% for the Gram-negative option and precision above 97% for Gram-positive ([Yu et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq249)). The refusal to guess is the catch. The same paper notes that PSORTb 2.0 returned a prediction for only about 50% of proteins in Gram-negative proteomes and about 75% in Gram-positive ones, and while 3.0 improved coverage, "Unknown" remains a large output class on novel sequences. CELLO ([Yu et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.21018)) and its GO-annotating successor CELLO2GO ([Yu et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0099368)) sit in the same era but ship as web servers with no source release, and the Chou-style Gpos-/Gneg-mPLoc family has no maintained implementation at all; the DeepLocPro authors note that implementations from that lineage "are often defunct and no longer available to the public" ([Moreno et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae677)). MetaLocGramN ([Magnus et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbapap.2012.05.018)) was the Gram-negative meta-predictor of that period and is both unmaintained and built on licence-encumbered components.

The second era is protein-language-model classifiers, and they are substantially more accurate per protein. DeepLocPro embeds sequences with ESM-2 and applies light attention, reaching overall accuracy 0.92 (archaea 0.88, Gram-positive 0.93, Gram-negative 0.91) in nested cross-validation; on a post-2010 benchmark where PSORTb's "Unknown" calls count as false negatives, it beat PSORTb 3.0 in every group, with accuracy 0.74 versus 0.34 for Gram-negative, 0.79 versus 0.50 for Gram-positive and 0.89 versus 0.70 for archaea ([Moreno et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btae677)). That comparison is harsher on PSORTb than a coverage-aware evaluation would be, but the direction is not in doubt. The newest entrant, LocPred-Prok, reports 91.2% accuracy and MCC 0.889 on a homology-partitioned benchmark with specific gains on Gram-positive cell wall and Gram-negative outer membrane proteins ([Zeng and Wang 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2026.169660)); it is distributed as a Hugging Face Space, with no packaged release and no licence I could verify from a repository, so it cannot anchor a production lane yet. mtx-COBRA pairs ESM embeddings with gradient boosting and was motivated explicitly by PSORTb's "Unknown" rate ([Arora et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2024.108114)), but likewise has no verifiable redistributable release. DeepLocPro's own repository carries CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which confirms your exclusion.

The gap this leaves is worth naming plainly: there is no maintained, permissively licensed, PLM-based prokaryotic localization predictor. The training data, however, is public. ePSORTdb curates experimentally verified localizations and cPSORTdb now holds over 66 million PSORTb predictions across complete and draft genomes ([Lau et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkaa1095)), and both DeepLocPro and mtx-COBRA were trained on ePSORTdb plus UniProt. Reproducing a light-attention head over ESM-2 ([Lin et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ade2574), MIT) or ProtT5 ([Elnaggar et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPAMI.2021.3095381), MIT) on those labels is a few days of work and yields weights you own and can redistribute.

### Membrane geometry and beta-barrels

If you decompose the question into membrane-versus-soluble first, the picture improves, because the open tools are strongest exactly there. TMbed predicts alpha-helical and beta-barrel transmembrane segments, their orientation, and signal peptides from ProtT5 embeddings, with no MSA and no Gram input ([Bernhofer and Rost 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-022-04873-x)). It is Apache-2.0, the ProtT5 encoder it pulls is MIT, and both can be baked into an apptainer image for offline nodes. Its published throughput is the only hard number in this survey: 20,375 human proteins in 46 min on the authors' server, 57 min for the same set on a desktop RTX 3060, and 566,976 Swiss-Prot proteins in 8.5 h, which is roughly 21,000 to 67,000 proteins per GPU-hour depending on length distribution. For two million ORFs that is about 30 to 95 GPU-hours, and the spread is dominated by mean sequence length, since attention memory grows with the square of it. Precomputed TMbed predictions over AlphaFold DB are browsable in TMVisDB ([Olenyi et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2025.168997)), which is useful for spot-checking novel lineages.

On CPU the permissive options for transmembrane helices thin out fast. SCAMPI2 is MIT-licensed and, critically, has a single-sequence mode that needs no profile, built on the same ModHMM engine PSORTb uses internally ([Peters et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btv709)); the wrapper scripts are Python 2 era, which is a packaging annoyance rather than a blocker inside a container. TOPCONS2 is GPLv2 and accurate ([Tsirigos et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv485)) but runs BLAST against UniRef90 per query, which rules it out at your scale, and CCTOP ([Dobson et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv451)) is a consensus server over components you cannot all redistribute. CINTHIA, the Bologna group's alpha-helical predictor, is GPLv3 but calls PSI-BLAST per sequence, so it is a per-genome tool, not a per-metagenome one.

Beta-barrel detection is the weakest link. PRED-TMBB2 ([Tsirigos et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw444)) and BetAware-Deep ([Madeo et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2020.166729)) are both web servers; the downloadable BetAware 1.0 code is GPLv3 but requires PSI-BLAST sequence profiles, and BetAware-Deep was benchmarked on only 15 TMBB proteins for topology and 1,009 TMBBs against 7,571 non-TMBBs for discrimination, so its error bars are wide. BOCTOPUS2 is MIT ([Hayat et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btw025)) but also profile-based. BOMP ([Berven et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkh351)) is fast and web-only, and predates the lineage diversity in your MAGs. HHomp detects outer membrane beta-barrels by HMM-HMM comparison and ships inside GPLv3 HH-suite ([Remmert et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkp325)), which makes it the licence-clean option, at HH-suite speeds. In practice TMbed's beta-barrel channel plus an HMM scan of known OMP families is the only combination that is simultaneously open, fast and lineage-agnostic; note also that AlphaFold2-based barrel detection works but is far too slow here ([Topitsch et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/prot.26552)).

### Sorting signals under an open licence

You framed the question as localization rather than signal peptides, but the soluble compartments cannot be split without a secretion call, so the licence situation matters. DeepSig is GPLv3, installable from bioconda or as a Docker image, and classifies each protein as signal peptide, N-terminal transmembrane, or other ([Savojardo et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btx818)). It requires the organism class up front via `-k {euk,gramp,gramn}`, so it inherits the Gram dependency. Two practical cautions: its bioconda recipe pins `tensorflow 2.2.0`, which no longer resolves against current conda-forge channels (the solve fails outright), so the Docker image converted to a SIF is the realistic install route; and because it reads the N-terminus, Prodigal ORFs flagged as 5'-partial must be excluded from signal-peptide calls. PSORTm dropped PSORTb's signal-peptide module for exactly this reason, since metagenomic fragments may not start at the true N-terminus ([Peabody et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa136)).

For lipoproteins there is no open replacement for LipoP ([Juncker et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1110/ps.0303703)) or SignalP 6 ([Teufel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01156-3)). PRED-LIPO ([Bagos et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1021/pr800162c)) and PRED-TAT ([Bagos et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq530)) are web servers. USPNet is the strongest recent organism-agnostic signal-peptide predictor and reports high sensitivity on poorly annotated organisms ([Shen et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-023-00576-2)), which suits novel lineages, but verify its repository licence before you containerize it. The pragmatic open fallback is PSORTb's own lipoprotein motif logic plus a PROSITE-style lipobox regex on the signal-peptide-positive set, which is a rule, not a licensed artifact.

Two integrative servers are worth knowing but not deploying. BUSCA runs separate Gram-positive and Gram-negative pipelines, using DeepSig for signal peptides, ENSEMBLE3.0 for helices and BetAware for barrels, with the barrel step applied only to Gram-negative input ([Savojardo et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gky320)). GP4 mimics Firmicutes sorting, adds cell wall interaction types and non-canonical secretion, and explicitly cannot predict outer membrane proteins for Actinobacteria ([Grasso et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bib/bbaa302)); it exists as a server plus a pipeline script that orchestrates third-party tools, so its effective licence is the union of its dependencies. LocTree3 reaches six-state bacterial accuracy of 89% plus or minus 4% but is profile-based and reports response times of about 90 s per protein on an unloaded server ([Goldberg et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gku396)), which is three orders of magnitude off your budget.

## Envelope typing from a MAG

### Taxonomy plus marker genes

Three of the surviving tools need a Gram call, so this is not an optional side question. The cheapest reliable route for MAGs is taxonomic: run GTDB-Tk v2 ([Chaumeil et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btac672)) and map the assigned GTDB lineage ([Parks et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkab776)) onto a curated phylum- and class-level envelope table. This is essentially what PSORTm automates: its envelope classifier combines curated phylum taxonomy with envelope-specific marker proteins, using Omp85 as the marker because it is the only essential outer membrane protein found in all classic Gram-negative bacteria ([Peabody et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa136), building on [Peabody et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv1271)). If you do not want PSORTm's container in the loop, the same logic reduces to a pyHMMER scan ([Larralde and Zeller 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad214)) over each MAG proteome for BamA/Omp85, LptD, LptE and the Raetz-pathway LpxC, calling diderm when two or more are present. Marker-based calls degrade gracefully on novel lineages in a way taxonomy alone does not, which matters when a third of your bins have no close relative.

### Phenotype classifiers and hard lineages

Phenotype-from-genome classifiers are the alternative, and they are weaker here than their headline numbers suggest. Traitar predicts 67 GIDEON phenotypes from Pfam profiles and does include "Gram negative" and "Gram positive" among its morphology traits, with macroaccuracy of 82.6% for the phypat classifier and 85.5% for phypat+PGL across all traits ([Weimann et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1128/mSystems.00101-16)). It is GPLv3 and on bioconda. The problem is the training set: 234 GIDEON species, overwhelmingly cultured clinical isolates, which is close to the worst possible prior for uncultivated environmental lineages. phenotrex, the successor to the PhenDB models ([Feldbauer et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-16-S14-S1)), is GPLv3 but last touched in 2021. microTrait ([Karaoz and Brodie 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbinf.2022.918853)) and MICROPHERRET ([Bizzotto et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40793-024-00600-6)) extract trait profiles from genomes but target metabolic and ecological traits rather than envelope architecture. For a Gram call specifically, marker genes beat trained phenotype classifiers because the genotype-phenotype link is direct.

The hard cases are hard for biological reasons, not computational ones, and no tool resolves them automatically. Diderm Firmicutes, the Negativicutes above all, carry a genuine LPS outer membrane and genomic evidence places that membrane in the Firmicutes ancestor rather than treating it as an acquisition ([Antunes et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.14589); [Taib et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-020-01299-7); [Megrian et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/mmi.14469)), with the Veillonella parvula outer membrane proteome characterized directly ([Poppleton et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2017.01215)). The outer membrane tethering systems themselves split along an ancient divide, with Terrabacteria and Gracilicutes using different machinery ([Witwinowski et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-022-01066-3)), so a marker set tuned on Proteobacteria will under-call diderms elsewhere; even recently described deeply branching Bacillota stain Gram-negative while remaining phylogenetically within the monoderm radiation ([Choi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1128/spectrum.00732-24)). Deinococcota have an outer membrane but stain Gram-positive, Chloroflexi are monoderm with atypical wall chemistry, and Corynebacteriales carry a mycomembrane that is neither classic outer membrane nor absent, which is precisely why PSORTb offers "Gram-positive with outer membrane" and "Gram-negative without outer membrane" as separate models. The current syntheses to calibrate your lookup table against are the phylum-level envelope survey ([Sutcliffe 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tim.2010.06.005)), the tree-wide envelope review ([Hashimi and Tocheva 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-024-01812-9)) and the Terrabacteria-focused one ([Beaud Benyahia et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41579-024-01088-0)), with the Thermotogae toga analysed separately ([Sexton et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2303275120)) and the monoderm-ancestor question still open ([Léonard et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/genes13020376)). Archaea sidestep all of this: they have no outer membrane in the bacterial sense, PSORTb and DeepLocPro both provide archaeal models, and the correct behaviour is to route GTDB-assigned archaea to the archaeal model and suppress outer membrane and periplasm as output classes.

## Recommended lanes

### Default CPU lane

Per MAG, run GTDB-Tk v2 and resolve an envelope class from the GTDB lineage, then confirm or override it with a pyHMMER scan for BamA/Omp85, LptD and LpxC over that MAG's Prodigal proteome ([Hyatt et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-11-119)), calling diderm on two or more hits. Per ORF, run PSORTb 3.0.3 from an apptainer image built from the Brinkman lab's prebuilt Docker image, passing `-n`, `-p` or `-a` from that call, and reach for the "Gram-positive with outer membrane" and "Gram-negative without outer membrane" models for Deinococcota, Negativicutes and Corynebacteriales. Build the SIF once on a connected node; the repository warns that a manual build now fails because the pftools dependency is no longer distributed. Backfill the "Unknown" fraction with a coarse membrane-versus-soluble call from DeepSig (gramp/gramn from the same envelope call, 5'-partial ORFs excluded) plus SCAMPI2 in single-sequence mode, and apply a lipobox regex to the signal-peptide-positive set for lipoproteins. Everything in this lane is GPLv3, GPLv3 and MIT respectively, so the whole container is redistributable.

The one number you need before committing is PSORTb's per-core rate, which is not published. Run a pilot on 50,000 ORFs across four or five MAGs of different envelope types and measure proteins per core-hour and the "Unknown" fraction per class; those two numbers decide whether PSORTb runs on everything or only on ORFs that the cheap membrane filter leaves ambiguous.

### Optional GPU lane

TMbed over all ORFs gives alpha-helical TM, beta-barrel, signal peptide and globular labels with no Gram input at all, at 21,000 to 67,000 proteins per GPU-hour, which is roughly 30 to 95 GPU-hours per two million ORFs. That resolves membrane-versus-soluble and inner-versus-outer membrane geometry on its own; it does not resolve cytoplasm versus periplasm versus extracellular, which still needs either PSORTb or a Gram-aware secretion call. Pre-download the ProtT5-XL-U50 encoder (2.25 GB) into the image, since the first run otherwise reaches for Hugging Face and your nodes are offline.

### Training a permissive predictor

If you want a permissive six-class PLM predictor, train it. ePSORTdb's experimentally verified labels are public, ESM-2 and ProtT5 are MIT, and both DeepLocPro and mtx-COBRA document the recipe: embed, pool with light attention ([Stärk et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioadv/vbab035)), classify into six locations, and optionally remap biologically impossible classes using the organism group. Partition by homology rather than at random, since the gap between DeepLocPro's cross-validated 0.92 and its benchmark 0.74 to 0.89 is mostly partitioning strictness. The result is weights under whatever licence you choose, at DeepLocPro-class accuracy, with no redistribution problem.

### Which lanes need a Gram call

PSORTb, DeepSig, BUSCA and CELLO require the envelope type as input. PSORTm, TMbed, SCAMPI2, HHomp and TOPCONS2 do not. DeepLocPro takes it optionally and uses it only to suppress impossible classes, which is the design worth copying: predict without it, then mask outer membrane and periplasm for archaea and monoderms. Supply the call per MAG, not per ORF, and carry it as a column through the pipeline so that a later taxonomy update re-runs only the affected bins.
