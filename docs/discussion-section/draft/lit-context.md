**Table observations to consider for the text**
- The table has only five rows whose "Structure from" entry is not "hand-written": APE/Workflomics, Wings, PROPHETS, SADI and LLM agents.

## 2. Candidate talking points by outline heading

### 2.0 Telling the reader what we told them (recap)

- Interoperability came from typing alone, between tools never designed together: 33 contracts, 79 types, zero routing code across three joined studies.
- Topology is derived rather than written. This answers the Intro's dataflow-vs-rule dichotomy:
  - Nextflow needs explicit topology [1].
  - Snakemake needs explicit filenames [6].
- Reproduced runs fell inside the rerun-to-rerun variability of the original pipeline.
  - nf-core/mag: 93.9% reciprocal MAGs vs 94.5–95.5% between reruns.
  - This is the right yardstick, not bit-identity (see Kim et al. 2018, doi:10.1093/gigascience/giy077 [in library]).
- Reproducing from a paper's methods text and scripts recovered 78.5% of published vOTUs (Pratama et al. 2026, doi:10.1038/s41467-026-68914-2). This places it among "reproduction from description" studies (§2.4).
- Joining workflows is where linear vs quadratic logistics cost shows. Integrating n tools pairwise needs O(n²) adapters; a shared vocabulary needs O(n).
  - The same argument motivates shared namespaces such as MetaNetX (doi:10.1093/nar/gkaa992) and EDAM (doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btt113 [in library]).
- Planning, typing and execution are kept separate, the way compilers separate logical from physical plans (§2.2, §2.3).

### 2.1 Limitations

- **No conditionals or loops.** This rules out iterative refinement: re-binning, iterative assembly, convergence loops, retry-on-QC-fail.
  - Contrast: Snakemake checkpoints [7], Nextflow full control flow [1], KNIME loops [19].
  - Possible mitigation: lift control flow into contracts, or into the solver's re-planning.
- **No ranking of redundant tools.** The solver returns *a* valid plan, not the best one.
  - Defensible: the choice needs domain expertise and benchmarks.
  - Related tool disagreement:
    - Binning: Han et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41467-025-57957-6.
    - Viral identification: Wu et al. 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03236-4 [in library].
    - GEM reconstruction: Hsieh et al. 2024, doi:10.1038/s41540-024-00384-y.
  - The same disagreement is the opportunity (see §2.2 multiverse).
- **Type quality is the ceiling.** Mis-specified or over-general types give valid-but-wrong plans.
  - Semantic composition systems hit the same problem: annotation quality is highly variable in bio.tools (Lamprecht et al. 2021, "Perspectives on automated composition of workflows in the life sciences", doi:10.12688/f1000research.54159.1).
- **EDAM gaps.**
  - EDAM is bioinformatics-centric, and its formats are mostly biological. Concepts for intermediate artefacts (bins, coverage tables, GEM solver settings) are thin or missing.
  - Metasmith's non-strict grounding is a pragmatic answer.
  - Sources: Ison et al. 2013, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btt113 [in library]; bio.tools registry, Ison et al. 2016, doi:10.1093/nar/gkv1116; Ison et al. 2019, doi:10.1186/s13059-019-1772-6.
  - A systematic quantification of EDAM's format gaps was not found [unverified].
- **Structural typing can over-match.** Two outputs with the same property set but different semantics will satisfy the same input.
  - Lineage constraints partly guard against this.
  - Compare nominal vs structural typing in Nextflow records [5] and CWL format IRIs [9].
- **Search cost and completeness.** MCTS is anytime and incomplete:
  - There is no guarantee of finding a plan when one exists, or of minimality.
  - Contrast: SAT-based synthesis in APE is complete up to a bound [25].
  - MCTS background: Browne et al. 2012, doi:10.1109/TCIAIG.2012.2186810; PUCT, Rosin 2011, doi:10.1007/s10472-011-9258-6.
- **Extra layer.** Python and a container relay add to Basic computing, plus an attack and maintenance surface.
  - Every abstraction layer adds a decay point; Taverna decayed with its services (Zhao 2012 [22]).
- **Reproduction was not exact.**
  - vOTUs reached 94.5% of the published count, with 78.5% recovered.
  - Sources of loss: undocumented parameters, tool versions, database versions, nondeterminism.
  - Cite Mangul et al. 2019 on tool installability and archival stability, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000333.
- **Simulated benchmarks.** CAMI II/III data are simulated, so performance on real communities can differ (CAMI II, Meyer et al. 2022, doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01431-4 [in library]; CAMI I, Sczyrba et al. 2017, doi:10.1038/nmeth.4458).
  - CAMI III publication status: [unverified].
- **Logistics LOC is a proxy.** Lines of code do not measure cognitive effort or maintenance. A user study (Loach's Usability and Learnability themes [L]) is absent.
- **Single-group evaluation.** Learnability and Support are untested. The community is small compared with nf-core [3].
- **Database dependence is invisible to types.**
  - Updating BiGG changed MEMOTE scores more than any tool change did.
  - Types do not capture reference-database version unless it is modelled as a property or lineage.
  - Sources: King et al. 2016 BiGG, doi:10.1093/nar/gkv1049; MEMOTE, Lieven et al. 2020, doi:10.1038/s41587-020-0446-y [in library].

### 2.2 Future directions

- **Tool ranking as a pluggable layer.** Let benchmarks (CAMI, Workflomics-style benchmarking [28]) or community votes weight the MCTS prior. This keeps the expert judgement explicit and out of the type system.
- **Multiverse analysis.** Since swapping tools is cheap, the system could enumerate all valid plans to quantify how analytic choices propagate to conclusions.
  - Precedents:
    - Neuroimaging many-analysts study, Botvinik-Nezer et al. 2020, doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2314-9.
    - Microbiome differential-abundance methods disagree across 38 datasets, Nearing et al. 2022, doi:10.1038/s41467-022-28034-z.
- **Learned policy and value networks.** The PUCT solver can take a trained prior, as in AlphaZero (Silver et al. 2018, doi:10.1126/science.aar6404) and retrosynthesis planning (Segler et al. 2018, doi:10.1038/nature25978).
  - Retrosynthesis is the closest analogy: MCTS over reaction templates from available molecules to a target.
- **LLM front-end, symbolic back-end.**
  - The LLM translates intent into target types, or drafts contracts from tool docs.
  - The solver guarantees well-typed composition.
  - This addresses hallucination and non-determinism in pure-LLM agents [34–38].
- **Auto-generating contracts.**
  - Sources to mine: bio.tools/EDAM annotations [27], Galaxy tool XML [15], nf-core module meta.yml [3], Bioconda recipes (Grüning et al. 2018, doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0046-7), BioContainers (da Veiga Leprevost et al. 2017, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btx192).
- **Lower to Nextflow typed records.** Nextflow 25.10/26.04 adds static types and duck-typed records [5]. Metasmith types could compile to them so that compiled pipelines are type-checked natively.
- **Additional back-ends.** Lowering to CWL, Snakemake or WDL would make Metasmith a front-end over several WMSs. This is the "logical plan, many physical runners" model of Apache Beam (Akidau et al. 2015, doi:10.14778/2824032.2824076).
- **Cost-aware planning.** Add core-hours and CO₂ as solver objectives.
  - Green Algorithms, Lannelongue et al. 2021, doi:10.1002/advs.202100707.
  - GREENER principles, Lannelongue et al. 2023, doi:10.1038/s43588-023-00461-y.
  - Carbon footprint of bioinformatics, Grealey et al. 2022, doi:10.1093/molbev/msac034 [in library].
  - Our MEGAHIT-only result (82% CPU-hours saved, 32% fewer vOTUs) is an example of the Pareto front the planner could expose.
- **Provenance export.** Lineage hashes map naturally onto Workflow Run RO-Crate (Leo et al. 2024, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0309210), CWLProv [11] and WorkflowHub (Gustafsson et al. 2025, doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04786-3).
- **Bounded control flow.** Add loops or conditionals as typed fixpoint contracts, or as solver re-entry after a QC gate.
- **Formal properties.** Coordination-theory results such as Kahn determinism and Karp–Miller coverability could give termination and determinism guarantees for compiled DAGs.
  - Karp & Miller 1969, doi:10.1016/S0022-0000(69)80011-5.
  - Kahn 1974, IFIP [unverified].
  - SyPet already uses Petri-net reachability for component synthesis (Feng et al. 2017, doi:10.1145/3009837.3009851).
- **Ecosystem-scale re-analysis.** At ~267 core-h per dataset, re-processing public metagenomes (for example all SRA groundwater samples) becomes a planning problem rather than an engineering one.
- **Database version as a type property.** This would make GEM and annotation results traceable to their reference version (see the BiGG finding).

### 2.3 Thoughtful comparison with what is already out there

**Coordination lineage (Intro §1)**
- CSP (Hoare 1978, doi:10.1145/359576.359585 [in library]), actors (Hewitt 1977, doi:10.1016/0004-3702(77)90033-9 [in library]), Kahn process networks [unverified, 1974] and Unix pipes [unverified, McIlroy] all fix the communication topology by hand.
- Metasmith derives topology from types. Its group-by and split routing is a restricted KPN, which may make determinism argued rather than assumed.

**Rule-based vs dataflow**
- Make [8] and Snakemake [6] chain backwards on filenames. Metasmith chains on properties, so it generalises Snakemake's backward chaining from string patterns to typed sets.
- Nextflow channels [1] make topology explicit. Metasmith generates that topology and compiles to it.

**Semantic and automated composition (most direct prior art)**
- APE / Workflomics [25–28]:
  - SAT-based synthesis over EDAM-annotated bio.tools.
  - Output is a set of candidate linear workflows for the user to rank.
  - Demonstrated in proteomics, not run at HPC scale.
  - Metasmith differs in four ways: DAGs with group-by/split, lineage constraints, MCTS, and compilation to Nextflow executors.
- PROPHETS / jABC "loose programming" [30,31]: the same idea (taxonomies plus synthesis), now inactive.
- Wings [29]: semantic constraint propagation over workflow templates, with Pegasus for execution. Templates still need to be authored.
- SADI/SHARE [32]: query-driven chaining of semantic web services. It decayed with the services (Zhao 2012 [22]).
- Perspective paper: Lamprecht et al. 2021, doi:10.12688/f1000research.54159.1. It names the barriers: annotation quality, ranking, execution.
- Classic AI planning for web-service composition (McIlraith et al. 2001, doi:10.1109/5254.920599) underlies all of these.
- Galaxy tool recommender [16]: a next-step suggestion learned from usage. It is statistical, not type-guaranteed.

**Type systems for workflows**
- BioShake [24]: Haskell type-level checking. It rejects bad compositions but does not synthesise them.
- Janis [unverified]: typed Python that transpiles to CWL/WDL.
- CWL [9]: File types plus EDAM format IRIs, which is nominal and shallow.
- Nextflow 25.10/26.04 static types and records [5]: duck-typed, a convergence toward structural typing.
- Flyte: typed tasks [unverified].
- Type-directed program synthesis in PL research:
  - Synquid (Polikarpova et al. 2016, doi:10.1145/2908080.2908093).
  - SyPet (Feng et al. 2017, doi:10.1145/3009837.3009851).
  - Metasmith is component-based synthesis over a typed library, the same problem class.

**Separating logical from physical plans (compiler and query-optimizer analogy)**
- Relational model and declarative queries: Codd 1970, doi:10.1145/362384.362685.
- Cost-based physical plan selection: Selinger et al. 1979, doi:10.1145/582095.582099.
- Volcano optimizer generator: Graefe & McKenna 1993, doi:10.1109/ICDE.1993.344061. Cascades 1995 [unverified].
- Multi-level lowering (LLVM, MLIR):
  - LLVM, Lattner & Adve 2004, doi:10.1109/CGO.2004.1281665.
  - MLIR, Lattner et al. 2021, doi:10.1109/CGO51591.2021.9370308.
  - Metasmith maps onto this: intent → typed plan → Nextflow DSL → executor.

- Omics tools: a large share fail to install, and URLs decay (Mangul et al. 2019, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000333).

**Virus-identification tool agreement**
- Real-world benchmark: 9 tools in 13 modes; TPR 0–97%, FPR 0–30%; tools find largely different contig subsets (Wu et al. 2024, doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03236-4 [in library]).
- "Gauge your phage": VIBRANT and VirSorter2 had the top F1 on artificial contigs; tool and parameter choice bias results (Ho et al. 2023, *Microbiome*, doi:10.1186/s40168-023-01533-x).
- Combining tools does not reliably help; VirSorter2 appears in every top ruleset (mSystems 2024, doi:10.1128/msystems.01105-23).
- Early in-silico viromics benchmark: Roux et al. 2017, doi:10.7717/peerj.3817.
- Tools:
  - VIBRANT: doi:10.1186/s40168-020-00867-0.
  - VirSorter2: doi:10.1186/s40168-020-00990-y.
  - DeepVirFinder: doi:10.1007/s40484-019-0187-4.
  - geNomad: doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01953-y [in library].
  - CheckV: doi:10.1038/s41587-020-00774-7.
  - MIUViG: doi:10.1038/nbt.4306.
  - MMseqs2: doi:10.1038/nbt.3988.
- Placement:
  - The original study's union-of-four-tools design inflates sensitivity, so small upstream differences propagate into vOTU counts.
  - That explains why 94.5% of the count can coexist with only 78.5% of identities recovered.
  - The press release reports ~257,000 vOTUs; the Nat Commun paper is doi:10.1038/s41467-026-68914-2.

- OPERA-MS without polishing recovered 21.4% of hybrid vOTUs, consistent with polishing being essential for long-read contigs [unverified, find a hybrid-assembly benchmark].

**GEM reconstruction quality and reference-database dependence**

- Uncertainty sources across annotation, database, gap-filling and solver: Bernstein et al. 2021, doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02289-z.
- Tools and resources:

## 3. Read these first (top 10)

1. **Loach, Smith & Bacon 2026, *Brief Bioinform*, doi:10.1093/bib/bbag396**: the source of the table's columns, and of the claim that Cost/Efficiency and structure production go unmeasured.
2. **Lamprecht et al. 2021, "Perspectives on automated composition of workflows in the life sciences", doi:10.12688/f1000research.54159.1**: closest prior-art manifesto. It names the barriers that Metasmith must say it overcomes: annotation, ranking, execution.
3. **Kasalica & Lamprecht 2020 (APE), doi:10.1007/978-3-030-50436-6_34, plus Kasalica et al. 2021 "APE in the Wild", doi:10.1021/acs.jproteome.0c00983**: the direct competitor. Covers SAT synthesis over EDAM/bio.tools and how it differs from MCTS plus Nextflow.
4. **Wilkinson et al. 2025, "Applying the FAIR principles to computational workflows", doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04451-9 [in library]**: positions the I in FAIR, and is already in the Intro.
5. **Zhao et al. 2012, "Why workflows break", doi:10.1109/escience.2012.6404482 [in library]**: decay framing, and the Limitations argument about an added layer.
6. **Nextflow static types and records docs and the 26.04 blog [5]**: Nextflow is moving toward duck-typed records. This must be addressed, and it may help the "additive" framing.
7. **Hsieh et al. 2024, doi:10.1038/s41540-024-00384-y, plus Bernstein et al. 2021, doi:10.1186/s13059-021-02289-z**: support the BiGG/database-dominance finding.
8. **Wu et al. 2024 (virus tool benchmark), doi:10.1186/s13059-024-03236-4 [in library]**: explains the vOTU partial recovery through low tool agreement.
9. **Segler, Preuss & Waller 2018, "Planning chemical syntheses with deep neural networks and symbolic AI", doi:10.1038/nature25978**: the best-known MCTS-over-templates synthesis precedent. It shows a learned prior is the natural next step.
10. **Samuel & Mietchen 2024, doi:10.1093/gigascience/giad113, plus Mangul et al. 2019, doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000333**: quantitative baselines for how often published computational work fails to re-run. They give context for the 78.5% and 93.9% numbers.
